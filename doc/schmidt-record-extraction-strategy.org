#+TITLE: Schmidt's Shakespeare-Lexicon: Record-Extraction Strategy
#+DATE: 2026-09-01
#+AUTHOR: Clifford Wulfman
#+OPTIONS: toc:t num:nil

* Status and purpose

Decisions, not findings. This document records the strategy settled on
in a Claude Chat session on 2026-09-01, following
[[file:schmidt-lexicon-reencoding-findings.org][schmidt-lexicon-reencoding-findings.org]], and supersedes that report's
framing of the problem. It is written for Claude Code as the primary
reader: it states what was decided, what evidence the decisions rest
on, and what constraints implementation must respect.

The findings report asked whether to re-encode ~20,000 entries from
scratch or to layer structure onto the existing flat corpus
incrementally. The answer is *neither*. Two discoveries changed the
question.

* Discovery 1: the P4 source retains what the P5 derivative lost

=Renaissance/Schmidt/copyright/schmidt.xml= (TEI P4, 21.8 MB) is
cleaner than =data/schmidt/lexicon/schmidt.lexicon.perseus-eng1.xml=
(TEI P5, 31.8 MB) in ways that matter structurally.

** Entry identity already exists upstream

The P4 source carries =<entryFree key="Abate" type="main">=:

- *20,101 entries, 20,101 unique =@key= values, zero collisions.*
- Homographs are already disambiguated by numeric suffix: =A1=--=A7=,
  =Abject1=/=Abject2=, and -- for the case the findings report
  identified as proof that homograph resolution is a real problem --
  =Bate1= (subst.) and =Bate2= (vb.).
- *1,922 headwords carry more than one entry*, covering 4,175 entries
  (~21% of the corpus).

The working P5 corpus has *zero* =@key= and *zero* =@xml:id=.

Open questions #4 (how do cross-references resolve when the target has
homographs) and #5 (what identity scheme do entries need) in the
findings report are therefore not open. Perseus's editors solved
identity and homograph disambiguation twenty-five years ago; a
transform somewhere in the P4→P5 chain dropped it silently. =Abate='s
=(cf. Bate)= resolves to =Bate2=.

** Italics were over-committed semantically

The P4 source has 19,525 =<emph>= spans. The P5 corpus has *exactly*
19,525 =<mentioned>= elements. A presentational signal (italic) was
converted wholesale into one semantic claim.

It isn't one thing. ~45% of those spans are multiword, and read as
uncited quotations or glosses (=the heels a the ass,=; =continue still
in this so good a mind,=), not as cited word-forms. 1,316 of them
contain the headword-elision shorthand (=--d=, =a.=), which is
otherwise a quotation marker. Italic in Schmidt distinguishes
quotations, cited word-forms, cross-references, and foreign matter;
=<mentioned>= is right for perhaps half of them.

** =<bibl @n>= in the source is not trustworthy

The findings report flagged an apparent hand-encoding error in
=abate.xml= (a Merchant URN on Shrew display text). It is inherited,
not introduced. The source reads:

#+BEGIN_SRC xml
<bibl n="shak. mv 5.1.137">Shr. Ind. 1, 137.</bibl>
#+END_SRC

*The display text is authoritative; =@n= is not.* This is exactly why
the completed citation-repair work (233,265 resolutions, derived from
display text) is the most valuable artifact in the project and must be
preserved as data rather than regenerated.

* Discovery 2: the lexicon is a database, not a text

The governing reframing. Schmidt's Lexicon is printed in two volumes,
but it is not prose, drama, or verse: it is a set of ~20,000 records
that happen to have been typeset. The "faithful encoding" instinct --
preserve as much of the source's form as possible -- is the wrong
instinct here. The goal is to translate printed form into structured
records.

The corpus measurements support this strongly:

| Measure                                   | Value  |
|-------------------------------------------+--------|
| Median entry length (text, chars)          |    130 |
| Entries under 200 chars                    | 12,652 |
| Entries over 5,000 chars                   |    216 |
| Long entries that are prose-heavy          |      4 |

Only four entries (=John=, =News=, =Obey=, =Presently=) are long
enough and citation-sparse enough to resist the record model. Even the
largest entry (~38,000 chars) is citation-dense. There is no
significant population of "essay" entries requiring a different
treatment. What does need a home in the record schema is Schmidt's
discursive residue -- asides like =used by none but Pistol= or =a
solecism formed in derision by Coriolanus= -- but that is a
field-design question, not an entry-genre question.

* The corollary: punctuation is syntax, not content

"We don't care about preserving the punctuation" is right in substance
and dangerous as a slogan. Much of Schmidt's punctuation is
*load-bearing as delimiter*. It must be parsed before it is discarded,
and no normalization pass may strip it before the parser sees it.

| Delimiter                | Function                          | Count / coverage                    |
|--------------------------+-----------------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| =:= (colon)              | separates =<def>= from citations  | holds in 11,012 of 12,992 entries with a =<cit>= (85%) |
| ~==~                     | gloss operator ("means")          | 7,598 instances                     |
| =( ... )=                | editorial aside / note            | e.g. =(used by none but Pistol)=, =(Ff /on/)=, =(cf. /Bate/)= |
| italic (=<emph>=)        | quotation, cited form, x-ref      | 19,525 spans                        |
| =--= + suffix            | headword elision (=--d captives=) | 19,652 tokens in 4,664 entries      |
| initial + =.=            | headword abbreviation (=a. the fire=) | ~70,700 tokens in 11,407 entries |
| =1)= =2)= / =a)= =b)=    | sense and sub-sense numbering     | 4,637 entries have =2)=; only 623 have =a)= |

The print form is the syntax of a record language. Parse it, consume
the delimiters, discard the tokens once they have done their work.

* The pipeline

Regenerate from the P4 source; do not continue mutating the P5
derivative. The derivative is a lossy intermediate, and every further
pass over it inherits the losses.

The move that makes regeneration safe -- and that dissolves the
"from scratch vs. incremental" dilemma -- is to extract the completed
citation-repair results into a standoff table *before* touching
anything else. The pipeline then becomes a pure, re-runnable function:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  schmidt.xml (P4, canonical, read-only)
    + citations.tsv     233,265 resolutions, editorially earned
    + gramgrp.tsv       ~25 abbreviation → pos/subc mappings
    + suffixes.tsv      ~25 dash-elision inflectional forms
    + xrefs.tsv         "cf. X" → @key, homographs resolved
    + senses.tsv        one row per sense (see below)
    ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    → generate (XSLT/XQuery) →  structured TEI P5
#+END_EXAMPLE

Consequences to hold onto:

1. *Nothing is destroyed by re-running.* Improve the sense parser,
   re-run, get a better corpus. The 233k citations stop being frozen
   inside a mutated file and become a data layer we own.
2. *The intermediate is the editorial artifact; the TEI is a
   serialization of it.* This inverts the usual assumption that the
   TEI file is what one curates. It is the right inversion here.
3. *The generator must stay stupid.* If =generate.xsl= ever needs a
   judgment call, that judgment belongs in an extraction column
   instead. Keeping the generator dumb is the test of whether the data
   has actually been extracted or the hard part merely deferred.

** Ordering

Identity first, cross-references last, because the latter depend on
the former.

1. =@key= → =@xml:id= restoration (positional join; both files have
   20,101 entries in the same order)
2. =<oRef>= conversion (dash-elision + letter-abbreviation)
3. =<gramGrp>= extraction
4. =<def>= / sense splitting
5. Cross-reference resolution

* Where this lives: =schmidt-lexicon-workshop=

The extraction work gets its own repository. The generated TEI is
delivered into =canonical-engLit=; nothing else about this pipeline
belongs there.

The reasoning is the MVP dataset framing applied consistently: a
canonical repo is a *published dataset*, a thing others build on. A
workshop is a thing that produces datasets. =senses.tsv= churning
through fifteen review passes has no business in the history of a repo
whose contract is "this is the corpus."

The negative evidence is close to hand. =canonical-greekLit= still
carries apparatus from its eXist days -- not because anyone decided it
should, but because the workshop had nowhere else to live, so it
fossilized in place. Today's archaeology on the P4→P5 migration was
archaeology on exactly that kind of fossil: an undocumented transform
that ate 20,101 =@key= values and left no record of itself. A separate,
versioned repo turns the transform from a lost event into a
re-runnable object.

** The cost, and how it is paid

Separating the workshop splits provenance: generated TEI would land in
=canonical-engLit= with no in-repo record of what made it -- precisely
the failure we are closing. Three mechanisms pay that cost. All three
are requirements, not suggestions.

*** 1. The generator stamps its output

TEI has the slot natively. Every generated file carries:

#+BEGIN_SRC xml
<encodingDesc>
  <appInfo>
    <application ident="schmidt-extract" version="0.3.1"
                 when="2026-09-14">
      <label>Schmidt record-extraction pipeline</label>
      <ptr target="https://github.com/PerseusDLCode/schmidt-lexicon-workshop"/>
      <note>generated from 4f2a9c1; source schmidt.xml sha256 e3b0c44...</note>
    </application>
  </appInfo>
</encodingDesc>
#+END_SRC

With the stamp, "where did this file come from" is answerable from the
file itself, permanently. Without it, the separation is a tidier
version of the same amnesia.

*** 2. Generated output is marked and the mark is enforced

A =DO NOT EDIT= note in the header is advisory. A =make verify= target
that regenerates into a temp directory and diffs against the committed
file is a proof. If someone hand-fixes an entry in =canonical-engLit=,
the diff catches it and the fix is known to belong upstream in a TSV.

This check is what keeps the inversion honest. Without it the TEI
slowly reacquires authority and we are back where we started, with
edits living in a 32 MB file and no way to re-derive them.

*** 3. The input is pinned, not moved

The P4 source is an edition artifact; it stays in
=Renaissance/Schmidt/copyright/=. The workshop vendors it -- submodule,
or pinned copy plus checksum -- and the checksum goes in the stamp.
That lets a future maintainer distinguish output drift caused by a
tooling change from drift caused by a source change.

** Naming and scope

The repo is =schmidt-lexicon-workshop=. The =-workshop= suffix names
the *relationship* to =canonical-engLit= rather than the subject
matter, which is the distinction that keeps the layout legible: it
tells a maintainer what kind of thing they are in before they read a
line of it.

Resist generalizing now. Abbott, Onions, and Dyce are the same shape,
but the extraction grammar is Schmidt's -- his colon, his ~==~, his
elision conventions. Build for Schmidt specifically. If a second case
proves the harness generalizes (the coverage-assertion machinery and
the TSV→TEI generator plausibly do), promote *that part* to
=corpus-tools= then, and =abbott-workshop= sits alongside. Designing
for reuse before there are two cases produces an abstraction shaped
like exactly one thing.

The =schmidt-tools= branch of =corpus-tools= migrates into the
workshop. Doing so is a useful forcing function for deciding what is
Schmidt-specific and what is general.

* The non-negotiable: provable losslessness

Because the print form is being discarded, we lose the ability to
eyeball an entry against the source. That check must be replaced by a
mechanical one.

*Coverage assertion:* for every entry, the concatenation of everything
extracted into fields, plus everything explicitly classified as
consumed-delimiter or declared-noise, must reconstitute the original
entry string exactly.

This is tokenizer discipline: the parser must account for every
character of its input. It runs over all 20,101 entries in seconds, and
it is the only mechanism that catches silent drops at scale. It is
also precisely the failure that produced the P5 derivative -- something
ate 20,101 =@key= values and nobody noticed for years, because nothing
was checking that the output still contained the input.

Every extraction pass ships with its coverage assertion. A pass that
cannot state what it discarded is not finished.

* Shape of the intermediate

Flat TSV for the closed vocabularies (=gramgrp.tsv=, =suffixes.tsv=,
=citations.tsv=, =xrefs.tsv=): greppable, diffable, editable as an org
table.

Entry records are nested, and TSV would fight that. Resolution: *one
row per sense, with a path column.*

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  key      path    pos    subc          def                                    note
  Abate    1              transitive
  Abate    1/a     verb   transitive    to beat down, to overthrow, to humble
  Abate    1/b     verb   transitive    to weaken, to diminish
  Abate    2       verb   intransitive  to decrease                            used by none but Pistol
#+END_EXAMPLE

Citations key on =(key, path, ordinal)=; quotations likewise, with
=<oRef>= positions recorded as offsets or as a lightly-marked string.
The nesting reconstitutes by sorting on =path=. The corpus stays
sortable, greppable, and reviewable in Emacs, and =git diff= over a
review pass shows exactly which senses changed -- none of which is
possible when the same edit lives inside a 32 MB XML file.

* Work split

Grounded in the counts above, this is far smaller than "20,000 entries
need hand encoding."

** Deterministic (no review queue)
- =@xml:id= restoration from =@key=
- =<lb/>= removal (established as noise by the sense-boundary survey)
- =<orth>= normalization (strip trailing comma)
- =<oRef>= conversion: closed suffix inventory (=s= 8,113, =ed= 3,996,
  =ing= 2,686, =d= 2,129, then =es/est/er/ies/n/st/eth= and a thin tail
  ending around =edst=), plus initial-letter match for the
  abbreviation form
- ~==~ → =<note>=

** Parse plus confidence-ranked review queue
- Sense splitting: 4,670 entries, of which only *623* have the
  two-level =1) a) b)= structure that =abate.xml= exemplifies
- =<gramGrp>= extraction from a closed vocabulary: =subst.= (1,930),
  =vb.= (1,762), =adj.= (612), =adv.= (199), =impf.=, =partic.=,
  =absol.=, =prepos.=, =plur.=, =pron.=, =trans.=, =intr.=,
  =interj.=, =conj.=, =compar.=, =fem.=, plus the prosodic
  =monosyll./dissyll./trisyll.= set
- =<def>= boundary via the colon rule (85% clean; ~1,980 entries with
  a =<cit>= fail it and need review)
- Cross-reference targets where the headword has homographs (1,922
  headwords)

** Editor required
Whatever the parsers flag. Tooling should emit a confidence-ranked
queue, *not* silently produce finished markup. A few thousand entries
of human attention, not twenty thousand.

* Corrections to the findings report

- The section "Even a single hand-encoded entry isn't fully
  self-consistent" describes a state of =abate.xml= that no longer
  obtains: there is no stray =/=, no =Lr. I I=, and sense 2's
  =<note>/<def>/<cit>= *are* inside =<sense n="2">=. It should not be
  cited as evidence of per-entry fragility.
- The =Shr. Ind. 1, 137= / =mv= URN mismatch is an inherited source
  error, not a hand-encoding error (see above).

* Open schema questions (not settled here)

These are for Cliff, and are underdetermined enough to be worth
Opus-level deliberation rather than implementation guesswork:

1. =<cit>= child order. =abate.xml= puts =<ref>= before =<quote>= in
   one case and after in others, and bare =<ref>= elements sit as
   siblings of =<cit>= when a citation carries no quotation. If child
   order is free, the ODD should say so; if not, normalize.
2. =<oRef type="nohyph">=. The distinction is recoverable from whether
   a word character follows, so =@type= is redundant -- harmless if
   documented, but it should be a deliberate choice.
3. Empty =<oRef>= with =@target= is a customization of TEI's
   dictionaries module. It belongs in the ODD family already under
   development, not in =tei_all=.
4. Where Schmidt's discursive residue lands in the record schema.
5. Homograph cross-reference policy: =(cf. X)= where X has two or more
   candidate entries -- rule plus review queue, or review always?
