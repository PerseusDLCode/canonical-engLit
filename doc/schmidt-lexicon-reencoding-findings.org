#+TITLE: Schmidt's Shakespeare-Lexicon: Findings on Re-Encoding Scope
#+DATE: 2026-09-01
#+AUTHOR: Clifford Wulfman
#+OPTIONS: toc:t num:nil

* Purpose of this report

Findings only -- no recommendation, no plan. Written to support Cliff
thinking through the scope of this problem elsewhere (Claude Chat)
before any further design or implementation work happens in this
repo. It documents what a single hand-encoded test case
(=data/schmidt/lexicon/abate.xml=) revealed when compared against (a)
the corresponding entry in the working corpus file
(=data/schmidt/lexicon/schmidt.lexicon.perseus-eng1.xml=) and (b) the
Schmidt print source it was transcribed from.

**Bottom line the evidence points toward:** the mechanical
citation-repair work completed so far (unwrap doubled =<cit>=,
collapse duplicate citations, strip edition tokens, recover line
numbers, merge orphaned citations, survey sense-boundary signal
strength -- see =doc/agenda.org= Phase 1) has been operating on a
*flat* text model. A single hand-encoded entry shows the actual target
structure is not flat, and getting from one to the other is not
another item in the same mechanical-transform sequence -- it looks
like a from-scratch re-encoding of the lexicon's ~20,000 entries.

* The test case

** The print source (image transcription)

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
*Abate,* (cf. /Bate/) 1) tr. a) to beat down, to overthrow, to
humble: /most —d captives,/ Cor. III, 3, 132.

b) to weaken, to diminish: /air and water do a. the fire,/ Ven. 654.
Tp. IV, 56. Mids. III, 2, 432 /(a. thy hours,/ = shorten). Merch. V,
198. Shr. Ind. 1, 137. H5 III, 2, 24. Tit. I, 43. Rom. IV, 1, 120.
Hml. IV, 7, 116.

c) to blunt, to take off the edge of: /a. the edge of traitors,/ R3
V, 5, 35. /from his metal was his party steeled; which once in him
—d, all the rest turned on themselves,/ H4B I, 1, 117.

d) to reduce in estimation: /I would a. her nothing,/ Cymb. I, 4, 73.

e) to deduct, to except: /a. throw at novum,/ LLL V, 2, 547.

f) to curtail, with /of/: /she hath —d me of half my train,/ Lr. II,
4, 161.

2) intr. (used by none but Pistol), to decrease: /and fury shall a./
H5 II, 1, 70. IV, 4, 50.
#+END_QUOTE

Every citation resolves correctly and every =<cit>= is well-formed --
this is the accumulated result of the whole citation-repair sequence
working as designed. But the entry is still, structurally, one long
run of text: the =1) tr. a) ... b) ... c) ...= sense/sub-sense
hierarchy exists only as an unmarked typographic convention inside
that text, "tr."/"intr." are bare abbreviation strings, the
definitions ("to beat down, to overthrow, to humble") are prose
sitting in the same undifferentiated stream as the citations that
follow them, =(cf. <mentioned>Bate</mentioned>)= is decorative text
with no actual link, and Schmidt's shorthand for citing the headword
inside a quotation (=--d captives=, =a. the fire=) is preserved
verbatim as print typography rather than encoded.

* Structural gaps this reveals

| Feature                      | Current corpus encoding                              | Hand-encoded target                                                          |
|-------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Orthographic form              | =<orth>Abate,</orth>= -- punctuation baked into the text | =<orth xml:id="abate-orth">Abate</orth>= -- clean form, own =xml:id=          |
| Sense hierarchy                | Unmarked typographic convention (=1) tr. a) ...=) inside running text | Real nested =<sense n="1">/<sense n="a">.../<sense n="2">=                    |
| Grammatical category           | Bare abbreviation text (=tr.=, =intr.=) inline           | =<gramGrp><pos>verb</pos><subc>transitive</subc></gramGrp>= per sense          |
| Definitions                    | Prose mixed into the same stream as citations            | =<def>= as its own element, preceding its sense's =<cit>= elements             |
| Headword-in-quotation shorthand | Literal print typography: dash-elision (=--d=) and letter-abbreviation (=a.=) preserved as text | =<oRef target="#abate-orth" type="nohyph"/>= -- one structured empty element for both conventions, disambiguated by =@type= |
| Cross-references between entries | Decorative parenthetical text: =(cf. <mentioned>Bate</mentioned>)= | Real link: =<ref target="#bate">cf <mentioned>Bate</mentioned></ref>=          |
| =<lb/>= (print line breaks)     | Present, inherited from the original transcription        | Entirely absent -- dropped as pure page-layout noise                          |

The last row is independent corroboration of a finding from the
sense-boundary survey (=doc/schmidt-sense-boundary-survey-report.org=):
that survey measured empirically that =<lb/>= correlates poorly with
sense boundaries (76.6% of entries have none at all; ~18% of existing
ones are mid-sentence print wraps). This hand encoding shows the
*intended target* independently agrees: =<lb/>= is not carried forward
at all.

* Complications confirmed beyond this one entry

** Homographs cross-reference each other, so linking needs a resolution strategy first

The corpus was checked for what "cf. Bate" should actually point to.
There are *two* separate =Bate,= =<entryFree>= elements in the corpus:

#+BEGIN_SRC xml
<entryFree><orth>Bate,</orth> subst., quarrel: <cit>
    <quote>breeds no b. with telling of discreet stories,</quote>
    <ref target="urn:cts:engLit:shakespeare.2h4:2.4.271">H4B II, 4, 271</ref>
  </cit>. </entryFree>

<entryFree><orth>Bate,</orth> vb., (cf. <mentioned>abate</mentioned>) 1) trans., a) to beat down, to
    weaken: <cit>...</cit>. ...</entryFree>
#+END_SRC

=Abate='s "cf. Bate" clearly means the second one (the verb), and that
entry cross-references back to =abate= in turn -- a genuine
bidirectional link between homographs. =abate.xml='s placeholder
(=<entryFree xml:id="bate"></entryFree>=, singular and empty) doesn't
yet reflect that there are two =Bate,= entries to disambiguate
between. Any corpus-wide cross-reference-linking pass needs an
identity/disambiguation scheme for homographs *before* it can resolve
a single =(cf. X)= mention correctly, and this =Bate,=/=Abate,= pair is
direct proof such cases are real, not hypothetical.

** The headword-in-quotation shorthand has more than one form

Two distinct print conventions were seen in this one entry alone:
dash-elision (=--d captives=, =--d, all the rest=) and bare
letter-period (=a. the fire=, =a. throw at novum=). =abate.xml=
collapses both into =<oRef>=, distinguished by =@type="nohyph"=
(present on the dash-elision cases, absent on the plain ones). Whether
these are the *only* two forms across the whole lexicon, or whether
there are others not yet catalogued, is unknown -- this entry is one
data point.

** The grammatical-abbreviation vocabulary is itself uncatalogued

"tr." and "intr." map cleanly to =pos=verb, subc=transitive/
intransitive= here. Other entries surveyed earlier in this session
used =subst.=, =vb.=, =adj.=, =adv.=, =trans.=, =intr.=, =absol.=,
=accus.= and more (see =doc/schmidt-sense-boundary-survey-report.org='s
false-positive discussion, which brushed up against several of these
without cataloguing them). None of that abbreviation set has been
enumerated or mapped to TEI =<gramGrp>= vocabulary yet.

** Even a single hand-encoded entry isn't fully self-consistent

Noted for completeness, not resolved: in =abate.xml=, sense =2='s
=<note>=, =<def>=, and =<cit>= sit as siblings *after*
=</sense>=, not nested inside =<sense n="2">= the way =a= through =f=
each hold their own =<def>=/=<cit>= internally. There's also a
mismatched citation target (line 50: =target="...mv:5.1.137"= --
Merchant of Venice's URN pattern -- attached to text reading =Shr.
Ind. 1, 137=, which is Taming of the Shrew), a stray =/= after a
closing =</cit>= (line 66), and a doubled capital-I typo (=Lr. I I, 4,
161=, line 86). None of this changes the structural picture above, but
it's a small preview of how much per-entry editorial attention scaling
this encoding to ~20,000 entries is likely to require, even with a
settled target schema.

* Scale

- 20,101 =<entryFree>= elements total in
  =schmidt.lexicon.perseus-eng1.xml=.
- The sense-boundary survey already found 4,690 entries with an
  unambiguous multi-sense marker (3,591 =clean=, 778 =partial=, 321
  =zero_lb_multi_sense=) that would need =<sense>= nesting. But
  =<oRef>= conversion and cross-reference linking aren't gated on
  having multiple senses at all -- a single-sense entry can still cite
  its headword via dash-elision or letter-abbreviation, and still
  carry a =(cf. X)= cross-reference. Those two transformations
  plausibly touch a much larger fraction of the corpus than the
  sense-nesting problem does, independent of it.
- =<gramGrp>= extraction depends on first cataloguing Schmidt's full
  grammatical-abbreviation vocabulary, which hasn't been done.

* What this changes about the work done so far

The completed citation-repair sequence (=doc/agenda.org= Phase 1,
items through =phase1/orphaned-cit-merge=) operated entirely within
the flat-text model -- unwrapping/collapsing/resolving/merging
=<cit>=/=<ref>= elements without touching the surrounding prose. All of
that work (233,265 resolved citations, 11,099 merged =<cit>= elements,
etc.) is still valid content -- the citations themselves aren't wrong.
But =abate.xml= shows the citations are only one piece of a much larger
target structure, and the =<sense>=-boundary survey
(=phase1/sense-boundary-survey=, closed 2026-09-01) -- which was scoped
as "the next step after citation repair" -- turns out to answer only
the sense-nesting question, not the =<gramGrp>=/=<def>=/=<oRef>=/
cross-reference questions this single hand-encoded entry surfaces.

* Open questions (not answered here -- for the Claude Chat discussion)

1. Is a from-scratch re-encoding of all ~20,000 entries the right
   framing, or is there a way to layer structure onto the existing
   flat text incrementally (sense-nesting first, then =<gramGrp>=,
   then =<def>=, then =<oRef>=, then cross-references) without
   redoing the whole entry each time?
2. What is the complete catalogue of Schmidt's headword-in-quotation
   shorthand conventions (dash-elision, letter-abbreviation, others?),
   and is it regular enough to convert to =<oRef>= mechanically, or
   does it need per-entry judgment?
3. What is the complete catalogue of Schmidt's grammatical
   abbreviations, and what's the mapping to TEI =<gramGrp>=/=<pos>=/
   =<subc>= vocabulary?
4. How should cross-entry references resolve when the target headword
   has multiple homograph entries -- and how common is that
   (=Bate,=/=Abate,= is one confirmed case; is there a way to survey
   how many headwords have more than one =<entryFree>=)?
5. What identity scheme do entries need (=xml:id= per =<entryFree>=,
   with homograph disambiguation) to support #4?
6. Given =abate.xml= itself isn't fully self-consistent after
   hand-encoding a single entry, is full automation realistic at all,
   or does this need a human-in-the-loop workflow -- and if so, what
   does tooling need to do to support an editor rather than replace
   one?
7. Does any of the completed citation-repair work need to be revisited
   under the new structure (e.g. =abate.xml='s =<cit>= for "Mids. III,
   2, 432" orders =<ref>= *before* =<quote>=, unlike the flat corpus's
   consistent quote-then-ref order -- is =<cit>= child order meant to
   vary, and if so does that affect any of the existing
   citation-processing tooling)?
