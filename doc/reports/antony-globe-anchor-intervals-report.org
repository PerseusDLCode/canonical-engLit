* Overview

Deliverable of [[file:../agenda.org::#phase1/rederive-antony-p5][#phase1/rederive-antony-p5]], the second pilot for the Globe
re-derivation pipeline built in
[[file:lear-globe-anchor-intervals-report.org][lear-globe-anchor-intervals-report.org]]. Antony and Cleopatra was chosen
specifically because its P4 source (=Renaissance/Shakespeare/opensource/ant.xml=)
has *zero* =<l>= elements -- all verse is encoded as =<p>=, the class of
play =done/interpolate-line-numbers= wrongly concluded had no Globe
apparatus at all (see that item's supersession note in =agenda.org=).
This pilot proves the pipeline generalizes to prose-encoded plays, not
just verse-encoded ones like Lear.

=corpus-tools='s =rederive-globe-lineation= command (unchanged in
algorithm from the Lear pilot; see below for the one attribute-handling
gap it needed) re-derived
=data/shakespeare/ant/shakespeare.ant.globe.xml= from the P4 source,
restoring every Globe and F1 =<lb>= as a =<milestone>=. This report is
the resulting anchor-interval closure audit: for every pair of
consecutive transcribed Globe line numbers within a scene, whether the
number of Globe boundaries actually falling between them matches the
arithmetic gap between the two numbers.

Full data: [[file:antony-globe-anchor-intervals.csv][antony-globe-anchor-intervals.csv]] (one row per closing
interval, one row per boundary of each non-closing interval). This
report does not resolve the non-closing intervals -- same as Lear's,
that is alignment-oracle territory, not this pilot's.

** Counts

Of Antony and Cleopatra's 7,198 =<lb>= elements in the P4 source, 3,570
are Globe (=ed="G"=, only 273 transcribed with a number) and 3,628 are
F1 (=ed="F1"=, all numbered) -- essentially the same density as Lear
(3,626 / 274), confirming the premise that this play's Globe apparatus
was never missing, just unreachable through =<l>=-based tooling. All
7,198 are now =<milestone>= elements; =check_corpus.py= confirms the
=citeStructure= line level reaches all 3,570 Globe milestones (leaf
depth =d2=3570=), against 273 before re-derivation.

The 273 transcribed anchors, taken 42-scenes-at-a-time, define **234
within-scene intervals**.

** Excess histogram (non-closing intervals)

"Excess" is the count of Globe boundaries actually found between two
anchors, minus the arithmetic gap between their values. Positive means
more boundaries were marked than the page's numbering allows for
(same dominant pattern as Lear -- split verse-as-prose lines given two
boundaries where the printed page counts one); negative means fewer.

An earlier informal audit of the raw P4 (=agenda.org=
=phase1/rederive-antony-p5=, before this task ran) estimated 236
intervals, 53 closing, negative excess ×3 -- close, but not exact
(same kind of drift documented in Lear's own report between its

counts, from the actual re-derived structure via the
=globe_anchor_intervals= module, are authoritative.

* Why intervals fail to close

The dominant pattern (178 of 181 non-closing intervals, excess
+1..+10) is the same mechanism Lear's report diagnoses: Greg's P4
markup emits a Globe =<lb>= boundary at every verse-line-as-typeset
break, including both halves of a shared/split line the printed page
counts as one Globe line. Antony's all-prose encoding does not change
this mechanism -- it only means every such split shows up as a =<lb>=
inside a =<p>= rather than a separate =<l>=. Not examined
boundary-by-boundary here, same as Lear's positive-excess cases; the
full text is in the CSV.

** The negative-excess cases (3 intervals) -- examined individually

Unlike Lear's 10 negative-excess intervals (already dispositioned in an
earlier phase), these three have never been looked at before -- the
collision/interval logic never ran on this file until this task, since
=strip-lb-tags.xsl= had nothing to transfer =@n= onto without =<l>=
elements to receive it. All three examined below using the CSV's
=leads_into= text.

*** Act I, Scene 2, anchors 41→50 (gap 9, 6 boundaries, excess −3)

#+begin_example
1: You think none but your sheets are privy to
2: Nay, come, tell Iras hers.
3: We'll know all our fortunes.
4: Mine, and most of our fortunes, to night,
5: shall
6: There's a palm presages chastity, if nothing else.  [anchor 50]
#+end_example

This is the Soothsayer scene's fortune-telling banter among Charmian,
Alexas, and Iras -- rapid one-line interjections, several under ten
words, one ("shall") a single word. Three fewer Globe boundaries were
marked here than the printed numbering (41→50) implies. Given the
density of very short exchanges in this exact span, the likely
mechanism is the *opposite* of the dominant +1..+10 pattern elsewhere:
rather than one printed line being counted twice, several short
interjections here were probably typeset sharing printed lines with
the interjection before or after them, and the P4 transcription marked
one boundary per typeset line rather than per speech -- undercounting
relative to what the marginal numbering 41→50 would need. Not
confirmed against the printed Globe page (out of scope for this
pilot, per its own deliverables list); flagged as the clearest
candidate for the alignment-oracle task to adjudicate first, since the
hypothesis here is falsifiable against Schmidt/Abbott/Onions/Dyce
citations landing in this span.

2: Go, you wild bedfellow, you cannot
3: soothsay.
4: Nay, if an oily palm be not a fruitful
5: prognostication,
6: Prithee, tell her
7: Your fortunes are alike.
8: But how, but how ? give me particulars.
9: I have said.  [anchor 60]

Immediately following the −3 interval above, in the same
fortune-telling exchange, with the same short-interjection density
("soothsay.", "Prithee, tell her", "I have said.") -- almost certainly
the same undercounting mechanism, one boundary short rather than
three. Also flagged for the oracle task; the two intervals sitting
back-to-back in the same speech-dense passage is itself evidence for a
single underlying cause rather than two unrelated transcription slips.

2: Why, this is to have a name in

4: reed that will do me no

Pompey's barge-party scene -- a different scene from the two above, so
not the same speech-exchange context, but the same shape: short,
broken clauses ("I could not heave.", "and not to be seen") rather

* Milestone placement: start-forward, not trailing

Unchanged from Lear -- see
[[file:lear-globe-anchor-intervals-report.org::*Milestone placement: start-forward, not trailing][that report's own section]] for the full account of why milestones lead
rather than trail the content they number, and the back-to-back-marker
bug (=_has_content_after=) that placement fix required. Antony's
all-prose shape exercises this exact code path more densely than
Lear's did -- Globe and F1 markers sit back-to-back constantly in
Antony's flowing =<p>= text (averaging roughly six =<lb>= per
paragraph across 1,173 paragraphs) -- and produced no new placement
bugs; the existing fix, and the new =TestProseRepositioning=/
=TestFidelityCheckHasTeeth= tests added for this pilot, confirm the
terminal-relocation and mid-sentence-relabel paths both behave
identically for =<p>= targets as they do for =<l>= targets.

* Coverage-discipline findings: one stray attribute, two new entities

Three P4 encoding details not anticipated in this task's own write-up
in =agenda.org= (which only knew of the two negative-interval buckets
in advance), each handled and reported per this project's coverage
discipline rather than silently dropped or worked around with a
one-off pre-clean of the source file:

- *One stray =<sp @n>=.* A single =<sp who="ant-1" n="20">= (Act III,
  Scene 6) -- =@n= is not a legal P5 attribute for =<sp>=. Mirrors the
  existing =<l>= stray-=@n=-stripped handling exactly (strip and log,
  don't raise, don't widen the attribute allow-list):
  =corpus-tools/src/globe_lineation.py='s =_convert_passthrough= now
  special-cases =sp= the same way =_convert_l= already special-cased
  =<l>=. New tests: =TestSpConversion= in =test_globe_lineation.py=.
- *Two new named entities.* =&aelig;= (lowercase; 280 resolved) --
  Antony spells "Caesar" as "C&aelig;sar" throughout, the lowercase
  form of the =AElig= entity Lear's =ENTITY_MAP= already had (Lear
  never needed the lowercase form; Antony needed it 280 times, 0
  uppercase-context collisions) -- and =&mdash;= (82 resolved), an
  em-dash Lear's P4 source doesn't use at all (0 occurrences there)
  but Antony's uses 75+ times for interrupted speech. Both added to
  =ENTITY_MAP= alongside the existing =AElig= -> =Æ= mapping; new
  tests =test_lowercase_aelig_entity_resolved= and
  =test_mdash_entity_resolved=.

None of the three required a schema change, a change to the
conversion's core algorithm, or a one-off pre-clean of the P4 source --
all fit the existing, established patterns for handling P4 encoding
oddities (log-and-strip for attributes, extend =ENTITY_MAP= for
entities) that Lear's pilot already established.

* Anchor-text-fidelity check

=agenda.org= =phase1/rederive-antony-p5= names, as a required
assertion carried over from the Lear pilot's own gap, that "every
transcribed number must still precede the same text it preceded in the
P4, checked for both reference systems... This belongs in the test
suite, not in a review pass" -- since Lear's existing structural tests
passed throughout while all 274 of its anchors sat one position out of
place. A new, generic (not Antony-specific) regression check,
=corpus-tools/tests/test_globe_anchor_fidelity.py=, derives the
expected leading text for every transcribed anchor directly from the
raw P4 source and compares it against the actual re-derived output.
Validated to have real teeth before being trusted: a synthetic test
deliberately reverts to the historical trailing-placement bug and
confirms the check fails against it
(=TestFidelityCheckHasTeeth=), then the same check is run for real
against both the already-shipped Lear P4/P5 pair (retroactive
regression guard -- passes) and this pilot's new Antony P4/P5 pair
(passes). Every one of Antony's 273 transcribed Globe anchors and
3,628 transcribed F1 anchors leads the same text after conversion that
it led (per its own trailing convention) in the P4 source.

* Out of scope

Restoring verse structure to Antony's prose encoding is real -- the
play is, after all, mostly verse typeset as continuous prose in this
P4 source -- but separable from this task, per =agenda.org='s own
scope note: citation resolution needs to know where the Globe's line
boundaries fall, not which lines are verse. Not attempted here. Also
out of scope, unchanged from the agenda: the other remaining plays,
resolving (as opposed to reporting) the non-closing intervals,
anything touching FTLN/=corpus-tools='s Globe↔TLN mapping utility, and
=@ed= ODD-level schema closure (deferred corpus-wide, per Lear's own
report).
