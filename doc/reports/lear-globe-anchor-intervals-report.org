* Overview

Deliverable of [[file:agenda.org::#phase1/rederive-lear-p5][#phase1/rederive-lear-p5]] (see [[file:alignment-oracles.org][alignment-oracles.org]] for the full
diagnosis and method). =corpus-tools='s new =rederive-globe-lineation=
command re-derived =data/shakespeare/lr/shakespeare.lr.globe.xml= from
its P4 source (=Renaissance/Shakespeare/opensource/lr.xml=), restoring
every Globe and F1 =<lb>= as a =<milestone>= rather than the
interpolated, collision-prone =@n= the 2025 migration produced. This
report is the resulting *anchor-interval closure audit*: for every pair
of consecutive transcribed Globe line numbers within a scene, whether
the number of Globe boundaries actually falling between them matches
the arithmetic gap between the two numbers.

Full data: [[file:reports/lear-globe-anchor-intervals.csv][reports/lear-globe-anchor-intervals.csv]] (2,385 rows: one row
per closing interval, one row per boundary of each non-closing
interval). This report does not resolve the non-closing intervals --
that is the alignment-oracle task (=phase1/alignment-oracle-lear=, to
be written), which will adjudicate them against Schmidt's Lear
citations. This is its input.

(=ed="G"=, only 274 transcribed with a number) and 3,301 are F1

elements in the re-derived file; =check_corpus.py= confirms the

anchors, minus the arithmetic gap between their values. Positive means
more boundaries were marked than the page's numbering allows for (the

* Why intervals fail to close

The dominant pattern (110 of 165 non-closing intervals, excess
+1..+4) is exactly the mechanism =alignment-oracles.org= §Layer 3a
diagnoses: Greg's P4 markup emits a Globe =<lb>= boundary at *every*
verse-line element, including both halves of a shared/split line that
the printed page counts as a single Globe line. Each such split line
inflates the boundary count by one without changing the anchor values
on either side of it.

** Worked example: Act II, Scene 4, anchors 160→170

Immediately following the collision this task set out to fix (Globe
160 = "Return you to my sister.", now correctly disambiguated from the
line before it -- see =alignment-oracles.org= §2), the very next
interval does not close:

| gap | boundaries found | excess |
|-----+-------------------+--------|
|  10 |                11 |     +1 |

The eleven lines between anchors 160 and 170 (King Lear's curse on
Goneril) contain no textually obvious split line in this span -- the
excess boundary is somewhere in "Never, Regan: / She hath abated me of
half my train; / Look'd black upon me... / ... / You fen-suck'd fogs,
drawn by the powerful sun" (full boundary-by-boundary text is in the
CSV, rows with =anchor_a=160, anchor_b=170=). This is precisely the
shape of problem =alignment-oracles.org= describes as tractable for the
oracle: not "which of 3,626 lines is which," but "which one of these
eleven boundaries is spurious."

** The −1 cases (10 intervals)

Ten intervals have *fewer* boundaries than the gap requires -- a
transcribed anchor was possibly mis-transcribed, or a Globe boundary
was dropped entirely somewhere the F1 milestones (denser, present on
nearly every line) may help disambiguate. See the CSV rows with
=excess=-1= for the full list; not analyzed further here, left for the
oracle task alongside the positive-excess cases.

* Milestone placement: start-forward, not trailing

An earlier draft of this task's output placed each =<milestone>= where
the P4 =<lb>= physically sat -- trailing the content it numbered,
matching Greg's own encoding. Cliff caught this against
[[https://www.tei-c.org/release/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/ref-lb.html][TEI's own =<lb>= reference]] (already the settled corpus-wide
convention for =<lb>= in this repo's =CLAUDE.md=: "marks the
*beginning* of the line it labels, not the end"). Checking =perseus_cts=
before implementing confirmed this isn't just a style question: the
resolver's =_milestone_chunks=/=elements_between= walk *forward* from a
matched milestone to the next one to build its cited span, and
canonical-greekLit's existing =card=-milestone =citeStructure=s already
place their milestones as a preceding sibling of the element they
introduce -- both assume start-forward placement. Trailing placement
would have made every citation resolve to the *next* line's text, off
by one, silently.

Fixed via a post-conversion pass
(=corpus-tools/src/globe_lineation.py=,
=reposition_milestones_start_forward=): each edition's milestone =@n=
values are shifted back by one boundary-slot (Globe scoped per scene,
since it resets there; F1 continuous across the whole play), and each
now-relabeled marker is physically relocated to lead the =<l>=/=<p>= it
describes -- not just left document-order-adjacent, genuinely renested
-- except a boundary embedded mid-sentence in flowing prose, which is
relabeled in place only (it already sits at the exact character offset
where its content begins; there's nowhere structurally to move it).
One new leading milestone was synthesized where a scope's very first
boundary was itself transcribed and had nowhere existing to shift
into: the F1 sequence's opening boundary (P4's =<lb n="2" ed="F1"/>=
immediately after the "ACT I" heading, before any scene begins). No
Globe scene needed one -- none of the 26 scenes' own first transcribed
boundary happened to be numbered.

A real bug surfaced and was fixed during this: Globe and F1 markers
routinely sit back-to-back in P4 (=<lb ed="G"/><lb n="311"
ed="F1"/>of what...=), so a Globe marker's own =.tail= is empty even
when real prose immediately follows its F1 neighbor. The first
implementation checked only the marker's own tail to decide "is this
mid-paragraph or a genuine line-end," misclassifying several
mid-paragraph boundaries as terminal and moving them -- which shuffled
document order and changed the interval-closure counts reported by an
earlier draft of this report (172 non-closing, spurious new −7/+7
buckets). Fixed by looking past adjacent same-position markers of
*any* edition to find the next real content before deciding
(=_has_content_after=, with a regression test,
=tests/test_globe_lineation_reposition.py=). The counts above are from
the corrected code and are stable: re-running the whole pipeline
reproduces them exactly.

* No schema change needed for P4's <reg orig> shorthand

An earlier draft of this task added =orig=/=reg= to
=perseus_base.odd='s core include list, converting P4's
=<reg orig="X">Y</reg>= (hyphenation-regularization shorthand, 12
instances in Lear, non-standard =@orig= attribute, not valid TEI P5)
to =<choice><orig>X</orig><reg>Y</reg></choice>=. Cliff judged this
unnecessary -- there's nothing worth preserving in keeping the original
hyphenated form separately marked up; the regularized reading is what
matters. **Reverted**: =perseus-schemas= commit =274d300= ("adds orig
and rec to allowable elements") reverted at =715787c=/=e6fd910=, and
the corresponding Schematron narrowing in
=corpus-tools/schematron/perseus_encoding.sch= (=legacy-reg= pattern)
reverted to its original unconditional form -- every bare =<reg>= is
flagged again, matching pre-task behaviour.

The re-derivation now collapses =<reg orig="X">Y</reg>= straight to
plain text =Y= (=corpus-tools/src/globe_lineation.py=, handled as a
special case in =convert_children= alongside entity resolution, since
it produces text rather than an element). No schema change, no
wrapper element, no effect on any other corpus. All 12 instances in
Lear verified to collapse correctly (=tests/test_globe_lineation.py=,
=TestRegConversion=).

The =@ed= closed-vocabulary change =alignment-oracles.org= calls for
(so a stray =ed="G"= fails validation) was **not** made: =@ed= is used
corpus-wide with dozens of distinct, legitimate sigils in
canonical-greekLit, canonical-latinLit and First1KGreek (Bekker, Spiro,
Loeb, actscene, oct, alt, ...), and =att.edition= is declared once in
=perseus_base.odd= for all of them. A hard closed list there would have
broken validation across three other corpora. Deferred; needs its own
design (most likely a per-genre or per-document scoping this toolchain
doesn't yet have a working pattern for -- see
=ODD-CHAINING-PROTOCOL.md='s note that a prior per-child attribute
override "never worked").
