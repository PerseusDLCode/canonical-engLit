* Overview

Survey for =phase1/bibl-survey= in [[file:agenda.org][agenda.org]]. Every =<bibl>= element
was extracted from all four Shakespeare reference works (via
=lxml.iterparse=, exact TEI-namespace tag match, streaming so the
30MB Schmidt file doesn't need to be held in memory) and classified
by whether it carries a machine-readable =@n= attribute and, if not,
by the shape of its text content.

The headline finding: **Schmidt is the outlier, not the norm.**
Schmidt's lexicon carries a near-universal =n="shak. <abbr>
<act>.<scene>.<line>"= attribute (99.99% of its 233,374 =<bibl>=
elements). Abbott, Onions, and Dyce do not — their Shakespeare
citations are almost entirely embedded as free text inside =<bibl>=
with no =@n= at all, in three different citation-string conventions.
Any Phase 3 resolver has to handle four distinct parsing problems,
not one attribute format with three copies.

** Summary

| Work    | Total =<bibl>= | With =@n=       | Without =@n=      | Note                                    |
|---------+----------------+------------------+--------------------+------------------------------------------|
| Abbott  |          5,205 |                0 |              5,205 | free-text only, mixed with non-Shak refs |
| Schmidt |        233,374 | 233,360 (99.99%) |                 14 | near-total =@n= coverage                 |
| Onions  |         33,292 |  2,996 (9.0%)    |             30,296 | =@n= is a minority overlay, not the norm |
| Dyce    |         14,388 |                0 |             14,388 | free-text only, ALL-CAPS play titles     |

* Schmidt --- =@n= format catalog (233,360 with =@n=)

Overwhelmingly clean. Format is always =shak. <play-abbr>
<locator>=, lower-cased, space after the period.

| Shape                        |   Count | % of with-@n | Example                  |
|-------------------------------+---------+---------------+---------------------------|
| act.scene.line (full)         | 204,766 |         87.8% | =shak. lll 5.1.50=        |
| act.scene only (no line)      |  15,732 |          6.7% | =shak. tn 2.5=            |
| single number only            |  12,708 |          5.4% | =shak. ven 200= (Venus & Adonis / Lucrece, cited by line only — correct for non-dramatic poems, not malformed) |
| sonnet, roman numeral + line  |      56 |        <0.1% | =shak. son cl.11=         |
| chorus/Gower special-cases    |       4 |        <0.1% | =shak. h5 4.chorus=, =shak. per 5.chorus1= |
| induction special-case        |       1 |        <0.1% | =shak. shr ind.2.77=      |
| **malformed: no locator**     |      53 |        <0.1% | =shak. luc= (bare, work only) |
| **malformed: duplicated token** |    18 |        <0.1% | =shak. ven ven= (data-entry bug) |

=@n= not starting with =shak.= (22 total, all legitimate — Schmidt
quotes the Bible and classical sources for etymology, not just
Shakespeare): =Hebrews 8.5=, =Plin. Nat. 19.19.1=, =Mark2.25=, =Hom.
Od. 19.394=, =Matthew 11.25=. A resolver needs a =shak.= prefix gate
before attempting CTS resolution; these 22 are out of scope for the
Shakespeare mapping table (=phase3/play-abbr-map=).

14 =<bibl>= without =@n= are Schmidt's own appendix cross-references
(=App. I, 1=), not Shakespeare citations.

**Total true malformed: 71 / 233,360 (0.03%).** Schmidt needs almost
no special-case handling.

* Onions --- =@n= is present on only 9% of citations

2,996 of 33,292 =<bibl>= carry =@n=, in the same =shak.= scheme as
Schmidt (evidently added by the same later editorial pass, sharing
even its bugs):

| Shape                       | Count | Example                        |
|-------------------------------+-------+---------------------------------|
| act.scene.line (full)         | 1,840 | =shak. h8 1.1.127=              |
| act.scene only                |   978 | =shak. ham 4.5=, =shak. wt 4.2.= (trailing dot) |
| single number only             |    70 | =shak. ven 405=                 |
| sonnet, roman + line           |    37 | =shak. son xc.4=                |
| **malformed: duplicated token**  |    23 | =shak. ven ven=  (same bug as Schmidt) |
| **malformed: no locator**       |    19 | =shak. luc=                     |
| Gower/prologue/chorus special  |    14 | =shak. per 3.Gower.36=, =shak. per 3.prologue= |
| **malformed: missing act (leading dot)** | 5 | =shak. shr .2.117= |
| **malformed: double-dot / blank play code** | 6 | =shak. rom 2.Chor..2=, =shak.  ..1674=, =shak.  4.7.80= |

**True malformed: ~52 / 2,996 (1.7%)** — small in absolute terms but
5x Schmidt's malformation rate.

The other 30,296 (91%) =<bibl>= have no =@n= at all:

| Bucket                    |  Count | Example                    |
|-----------------------------+--------+------------------------------|
| has digit (locator-bearing) | 29,391 | =Ham. II. i. 58=, =LLL. V. ii. 17= |
| no digit, short              |    905 | =iii.=, =Wint. IV. iii.= (elliptical continuation refs — omit the play when it repeats the previous entry's) |

Format for the 29,391: =<PlayAbbr>. <Act roman>. <scene roman>.
<line arabic>=, title-cased abbreviation, periods after each
segment. This is the dominant Onions format by a wide margin — a
resolver for Onions is mostly a free-text parser, with the 2,996
=@n= entries as a bonus, not the primary path.

* Dyce --- no =@n= at all; free text only (14,388)

| Bucket                    |  Count | Example                                          |
|-----------------------------+--------+----------------------------------------------------|
| has digit (locator-bearing)  | 12,032 | =THE TWO GENTLEMEN OF VERONA, i. 2. 53=            |
| no digit, short (editor tag) |  2,159 | =MALONE=, =(STEEVENS)=, =(BLACKSTONE)=, =(MASON)= |
| no digit, long (other biblio) |    197 | =Latham's ed. of Johnson's Dict.=, =Forby's Vocab. of East Anglia.= |

Format for the 12,032: **full play title in ALL CAPS**, comma, lower
roman act, lower roman scene, arabic line — the most verbose and
most consistent of the three free-text conventions (no
abbreviation-table needed, but title strings must be matched against
full canonical titles, not a short-code lookup).

The 2,159 "editor tag" bibls are not Shakespeare citations at all —
they're attributions naming which 18th/19th-century editor
(Steevens, Malone, Farmer, Warburton, Mason, Blackstone...)
contributed the adjoining note. A resolver must recognize and skip
these, not attempt to parse them as locators.

* Abbott --- no =@n=; free text, and citations are the minority of =<bibl>= usage (5,205)

Abbott's =<bibl>= is used for two structurally different things, and
naive "has a digit" bucketing conflates them:

1. **External bibliographic citations** (footnotes/front matter
   citing other 19th-century scholarship): e.g. =MÄTZNER'S Englische
   Grammatik (3 vols., Berlin, 1865)=, =William of Palerne (Early
   English Text Society, 1867)=. These have digits (dates, volume
   counts) but are never Shakespeare locators.
2. **Genuine Shakespeare (and other Elizabethan-author) passage
   citations**, e.g. =Macbeth, i. 4. 11.=, =Rich. III. iii. 3. 23=,
   =J. C. iv. 3. 102=, poems cited by line only (=V. and A. 604=,
   =R. of L. 404=, =Sonn. 146=). A meaningful minority cite
   *non-Shakespeare* Elizabethan authors for comparison (=B. J.
   Sejan. iii. 1.= — Ben Jonson's Sejanus; =SPEN. F.Q. i. 5. 42.= —
   Spenser's Faerie Queene; Bible, Bacon, Montaigne, Pope) — these
   need to be excluded from the Shakespeare map, not mis-resolved.
3. A separate 154 =<bibl>= are the front-matter index of "fully
   indexed" play titles with no locator at all (=<bibl>Hamlet</bibl>=)
   — not citations, just a list.

Because (1) and (2) are both "has a digit" and share no reliable
syntactic marker (no @n, no consistent parenthesization — most of
the =<cit><bibl>...</bibl></cit>= examples are bare, only 7 of 5,205
happen to be parenthesized), separating them mechanically needs a
**play/author abbreviation whitelist**, not a regex on shape alone.
This is a materially harder parsing problem than Onions or Dyce, and
should be budgeted accordingly for =phase1/citestructure-refworks=
and =phase3/bibl-resolver=. Abbott was not fully sub-classified here
— building an authoritative abbreviation whitelist is Phase 3 work
(=phase3/play-abbr-map=), not this survey.

* Implications for Phase 3 resolver design

- Four different citation grammars, not one: Schmidt's =@n= scheme
  is the easy case (also used by ~9% of Onions); the other three
  free-text conventions all need dedicated parsers:
  - Onions: =Abbr. RomanAct. RomanScene. ArabicLine.=
  - Dyce: =FULL TITLE, roman.roman.arabic=
  - Abbott: =Abbr, roman.roman.arabic= or =Abbr line= for poems,
    *mixed in with non-Shakespeare citations requiring a
    whitelist to exclude*.
- A single play/poem-abbreviation → CTS-URN mapping table
  (=phase3/play-abbr-map=) needs to cover at least three distinct
  abbreviation vocabularies (Schmidt/Onions share one; Abbott and
  Dyce each use their own), plus Dyce's full-title strings.
- Malformation is real but small everywhere it was measured
  (Schmidt 0.03%, Onions 1.7% of their =@n= entries) — not a
  blocker, just needs a "give up gracefully, don't guess" fallback
  in the resolver, consistent with [[file:agenda.org][agenda.org]]'s existing stance on
  data errors.
- Editor-attribution and non-locator bibls (Dyce's 2,159, Onions'
  905 elliptical refs, Abbott's 154-entry play index) must be
  recognized and *skipped*, not treated as failed parses.
- Onions and Dyce did not validate against =perseus_lexical.rng=
  when the CTS structure was added ([[file:agenda.org][agenda.org]],
  =phase1/cts-structure-onions-dyce=, ~7,000 jing errors) — that
  content-encoding repair is independent of this citation-parsing
  work but will need to happen before or alongside
  =phase1/citestructure-refworks=.
